# Kitab Al-Furqan

## Book of Proof

Kitab Al-Furqan is an Islamic book written by **Furkan Selim Kucukaslan**.

The book explores Islam through arguments, evidence, scripture, philosophy, history, and comparative religion. It discusses major questions about God, revelation, atheism, Christianity, Judaism, Hinduism, Buddhism, and the foundations of Islamic belief.

The goal of the book is simple: to examine different worldviews and present arguments for why Islam is true.

## Chapters

The current book is divided into six main chapters:

1. **Al-Masihiyyah**
   Christianity, the teachings of Jesus, the Trinity, Biblical passages, and Christian theology.

2. **Al-Ilhad**
   Atheism, naturalism, arguments for God's existence, reason, morality, consciousness, cosmology, and philosophical objections to atheism.

3. **Al-Yahudiyyah**
   Judaism, the Hebrew Bible, Biblical narratives, textual questions, and comparisons with Islamic teachings.

4. **Al-Hindusiyah**
   Hinduism, polytheism, Hindu scriptures, cosmology, idols, and philosophical differences between Hindu thought and Islamic monotheism.

5. **Al-Budhiyyah**
   Buddhism, its understanding of God, reincarnation, morality, and its philosophical differences from Islam.

6. **Al-Islam**
   Islamic theology, Tawhid, the Quran, Islamic teachings, and arguments supporting the Islamic worldview.

The chapter structure is implemented directly in the digital version of the book.

## Sources

The book includes a dedicated sources section for information used throughout the work. The digital version provides a separate page explaining where the information in the book comes from.

The intention is to make the arguments easier to examine instead of simply asking the reader to accept them without investigation.

## Purpose

Kitab Al-Furqan was created as an attempt to present Islamic belief through a combination of religious texts, philosophical reasoning, historical discussion, and comparative analysis.

It is written from an Islamic perspective and is intended primarily for readers interested in understanding arguments for Islam and examining objections raised against it.

The book does not aim to hide disagreement. Christianity, atheism, Judaism, Hinduism, and Buddhism are discussed directly, while the final chapter focuses on Islam and its theological foundations.
